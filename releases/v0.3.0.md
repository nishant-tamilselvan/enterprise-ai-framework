# Enterprise AI Framework v0.3.0

**Release date:** 2026-09-28  
**Status:** Draft  
**License:** CC BY 4.0 (content), MIT (code)

## Executive summary

This release rebuilds the compliance mappings and the security section against current
sources, checked on 2026-09-28. Every mapping now cites article, clause or category
identifiers and carries a standard disclaimer. The security pages name threats with OWASP
LLM Top 10 2026, OWASP Agentic Top 10 and MITRE ATLAS identifiers. Two new pages cover the
AI supply chain and incident response. The Delivery section gains a reference
implementation, and the site gains section tabs and diagrams you can zoom.

## Highlights

- The EU AI Act mapping reflects the Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744),
  which moved high-risk obligations to 2027-12-02 for Annex III and 2028-08-02 for Annex I.
- The NIST AI RMF mapping covers all 19 categories, the 7 trustworthiness characteristics
  and the 12 risks in the Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1).
- The ISO/IEC 42001 mapping covers clauses 4 to 10 and all 38 Annex A controls.
- The FedRAMP mapping covers the Consolidated Rules for 2026, Key Security Indicators and
  the SP 800-53 control families.
- New security pages: AI supply chain, and AI incident response.
- A reference implementation page for the delivery model, and a starter standards library
  in the format AEGIS reads.

## Detailed changes

### Added

- AI supply chain page: inventory, AI bills of materials, model signing, supplier risk.
- AI incident response page: incident types, response steps, regulatory reporting and
  information sharing.
- A shared disclaimer on every compliance mapping page.
- A reference implementation page for the delivery model, using AEGIS, with its gaps stated.
- A starter standards library in `standards/`, built from six framework rules.
- An MIT license for code: scripts, theme overrides, workflows and configuration. Content
  stays under CC BY 4.0.

### Changed

- EU AI Act mapping: roles, the amended timeline, Articles 4, 5 and 50, provider and
  deployer obligations for high-risk systems (Articles 9 to 17, 26 and 27), serious
  incident reporting (Article 73), penalties, Commission guidance and standards status.
- NIST AI RMF mapping: category-level mapping, trustworthiness characteristics, the
  Generative AI Profile, and related NIST work such as the draft Cyber AI Profile.
- ISO/IEC 42001 mapping: clause and Annex A mapping, and related standards including
  ISO/IEC 42005 and 42006.
- FedRAMP mapping: CR26, certification classes, Key Security Indicators, SP 800-53 Rev. 5
  Release 5.2.0 families, NIST's planned AI control overlays, and OMB memoranda.
- Threat model: trust boundaries, a threat catalog with OWASP LLM Top 10 2026, OWASP
  Agentic Top 10 and MITRE ATLAS v2026.09 identifiers, and a crosswalk from the 2025 to
  the 2026 OWASP LLM IDs. New sections on improper output handling, excessive agency and
  the Rule of Two, MCP tool poisoning, and hidden context exposure.
- Zero-trust AI: enforcement outside the model, agent identity and MCP authorization.
- Data protection: where AI copies data, and the joint guidance on securing AI data.
- Site: one navigation tab per section, breadcrumbs, page icons and metadata chips.
  Diagrams open in a full-screen viewer with zoom and pan.
- Spelling: the Delivery pages and three figures now use American spelling, and the ECA
  wheel reads "ECA sits at the heart".

### Deprecated or removed

- None.

### Security and safety

- The security section now covers improper output handling, excessive agency and
  multi-agent risk, MCP and tool poisoning, AI supply chain integrity, hidden context
  exposure, and incident response.

## Architecture impact

Trust boundaries are now explicit in the threat model, including agent-to-tool and
agent-to-agent boundaries. The zero-trust page requires security decisions to run in code
outside the model.

## Assurance and compliance impact

The mappings trace to identifiers that auditors and regulators use. They remain
informative. They do not provide legal advice, certification, authorization or conformity
assessment.

## Compatibility and migration

Teams that planned for EU AI Act high-risk obligations on 2026-08-02 should move those
plans to the amended dates. Page structure changed on every compliance and security page,
so update any deep links to old headings.

## Known limitations

- NIST is revising the AI RMF, and the category titles may change.
- NIST's AI control overlays for SP 800-53 are not yet published.
- No harmonised standards for the EU AI Act are cited in the Official Journal yet.
- The standards library covers six rules and is marked Draft. Organizations review it and approve it in their own library.
